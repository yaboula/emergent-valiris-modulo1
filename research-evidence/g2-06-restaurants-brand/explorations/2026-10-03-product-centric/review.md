# Discussion notes — author visual review

These are visual conversation pieces, not a selected system or a PM proposal. The next conversation may borrow, discard or combine any part. No palette, font, character shape, layout or model count is locked by these images.

## What the first three generations reveal

The first sketch has the strongest immediate graphic energy: huge sans headline, cropped red field, centered offer surface and characters directing attention. Its generated tabletop, paper tent and food photographs shifted the requested digital product toward a physical menu. A focused refinement keeps the composition and subjects while exploring a clean digital surface, flat 2D acting and selective UI elevation.

The second sketch gives the typography and illustration different scales, with an asymmetric editorial gutter and orange field. It leans toward serif print and tactile 3D despite the requested graphic 2D language. It is useful as an editorial contrast, not proof that those generated details belong to the final identity. Its initial generation invented supporting copy about designing spaces and experiences. That copy is inaccurate for the established offer and is superseded by a targeted text edit. The original is archived as process evidence, not reusable marketing material.

The third gives a useful sense of warm light, spatial hierarchy and matte depth. The lemon/fork subjects demonstrate that the open repertoire can extend beyond the original four. Its folded paper object, natural texture and serif lettering also pull it toward a physical restaurant still-life. It is an atmospheric reference within this exploration, not an approved physical product or the preferred digital UI direction.

## Working judgement

The refined first image was inspected: its rough tent-menu and realistic props are removed; the central surface now floats with a restrained shadow, category fields are graphic illustrations, type is sans and tomato/spoon acting is closer to the flat family. Slight shading remains in the generated drawing; it is not a locked illustration rule. Category art is illustrative and does not establish actual menu contents. The refined second image was inspected: the invented spaces/experiences paragraph is replaced by the exact exploratory sentence, with the scene retained. All three final specimens retain the conceptual/non-definitive boundary. No final design selection is inferred from producing them.

The promising combination is the first sketch's graphic energy and product prominence, a controlled amount of the third's light/depth, and the second's editorial rhythm. A professional identity still needs deliberate lettering, coherent character production and a chosen application. Soft depth can provide hierarchy through overlap, elevation and contact shadow; it need not turn the whole interface or every character into a rendered object.

The characters should perform a readable action in relation to the product or content. Their shared visual character is a starting point that can develop, not a rigid cast or immutable anatomy. A giant character can occasionally own a composition; other moments can use a cropped gesture or no character. This exploration does not set interface behaviours or require motion.

## Limits

Titles, navigation and interface content are raster generation, not editable UI or verified interaction. Fonts are not identified; these samples do not define licensed production font choices. Fictional “Casa Naranja”, invented menu illustrations/food photographs and character objects do not depict an actual venue or its offered dishes. No accessibility, localisation, recognition, conversion, customer or legal/exclusivity result is established. Real menu values, food information, client hierarchy and production should be addressed under the eventual authorised brief.
