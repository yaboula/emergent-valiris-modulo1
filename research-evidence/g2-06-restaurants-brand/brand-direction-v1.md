# Valiris Restaurants — proposed brand direction

3 October 2026 · G2.06 · v1.0 · **Proposal delivered; identity and brand hierarchy not approved.**

**Recommend A, “Hacer sitio”, with R1, restaurant-led identity and a discreet line endorsement.** The line acts as a host: it gives each restaurant room to express itself and gives essential information room to be read. An asymmetric fold, expressive type and a selectively used paper character make that idea visible. On client surfaces the restaurant remains the identified business; the exact accepted attribution is **“Powered by Valiris Restaurants”**.

This is a direction decision package, not production branding, a Storefront implementation or an approved model family. Three identity alternatives do not mean three base models. The current intention remains two or three base models, with exact families/count/setup limits pending, plus genuinely bespoke studio design.

## 1. Identity strategy and its evidence boundary

Valiris Restaurants should express **welcoming precision**: generous in how it introduces a restaurant, exact in what it says about the offer. “Hacer sitio” gives this a concrete rule: the studio makes room for the business, the diner’s intention and the restaurant’s daily changes. The folded-paper gesture can become an expressive line signature without being attached to every product or price. Character comes from a hospitable gesture and confident composition; it does not require a smiling ingredient or a permanent mascot.

The intended audience is independent restaurants and cafés in Spain, and their diners, as declared in the project sources. For the diner this direction seeks recognition and unimpeded reading; for the restaurant it seeks representation that can survive ordinary content changes; for Valiris it seeks a reusable craft vocabulary with a defined bespoke route. These are design intentions. The product decisions establish studio-controlled design, content autonomy, public consultation without accounts and separate expressive/consultation intensities; they do not prove easier maintenance, increased sales, faster choice, margin or a market advantage.

The proposed line message is **“Cada local tiene su sitio.”** It describes the concept, not a contractual service promise. Supporting draft copy: “Presencia y carta con carácter. Contenido al cuidado de tu restaurante.” G2.04/G6 must refine the latter against what is actually deliverable. There is no approved slogan, restaurant copy system, tariff, unlimited-support promise or legal name clearance.

A concrete positioning hypothesis is a studio that treats the restaurant’s representation and maintained offer as one professional design responsibility. That fits the accepted template/bespoke model. It is not a demonstrated exclusive differentiator. A stronger commercial claim needs G6 and observable delivery/participant evidence.

## 2. Three levels and three relationship alternatives

Keep **Valiris** as the umbrella studio and **Valiris Restaurants** as the unchanged line/product name under ADR-0002/0003. Restaurants remain separately named businesses. The sources do not supply an approved umbrella logo system, so this proposal does not invent one or apply the restaurant character to every future Valiris vertical.

| Level | Proposed visual and verbal job | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valiris | Identify the studio in ownership, proposals and studio context; explain the relationship in prose when relevant | No new umbrella identity, commercial entity or rename |
| Valiris Restaurants | Full named lockup on line-facing documents, future commercial material and relevant product/service identity; selective fold/character vocabulary | Never “Valiris” alone as the product; do not translate Restaurants into a new product name |
| Restaurant | First identifiable business on its public surfaces, with its name, supplied mark, maintained facts and local expression | A restaurant is not a department of Valiris or an interchangeable platform member |

Sheet 04 compares the same fictional restaurant, entry label and attribution.

| Relationship | What changes | Benefit and maintenance consequence | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **R1 / Restaurant-led endorsement** | Restaurant name/mark is primary; line is a secondary signature, separate from essential facts | Allows local recognition and bespoke expression. Requires a restrained endorsement variant and coexistence checks. Valiris gets less visual exposure on client pages | **Choose R1** |
| R2 / Visible co-branding | Both names have comparable graphic weight | More visible provider presence; competing identities consume space, especially with long names. Each custom client needs a clear coexistence agreement | Use only for a consciously co-branded programme if the founder later chooses one |
| R3 / Line-led platform | Valiris Restaurants is primary, venue becomes secondary | Strong provider consistency and simpler shared presentation; risks obscuring which business the diner is consulting and reducing local distinctiveness | Do not choose for the current independent-restaurant studio proposition |

Provider exposure is observable in a specimen; recognition, recall and conversion are unmeasured. R1 follows the product’s restaurant-representation purpose. The founder’s choice of a department as the branding subject does not approve this public hierarchy.

**Attribution rule proposed for R1:** retain the exact accepted wording, in readable text, in a secondary credit area determined by IA/UI. Do not turn it into a mandatory introductory gateway, large competing logo or character next to a price. On narrow space it may wrap without abbreviating Valiris Restaurants. Do not introduce “by Valiris”, “Made with…” or a translated replacement silently. Placement, linking destination and whether a small symbol accompanies the wording need later application decisions; no live destination is invented here.

A changed price must not be an outlined illustration or part of a manually redrawn poster. New composition, character commissions or identity changes are separately scoped studio work, not the prerequisite for changing everyday content.

## 3. Distinct identity proposals

All three keep the same name. Their Spanish titles are internal creative territories, not replacement product names or restaurant sub-brands. The type-set lockups are concept specimens, not finished custom-lettered logos.

### A / Hacer sitio — recommended

**Meaning:** hosting as making room. A folded object can open, gather and gesture; it connects the studio’s design work with hospitality without claiming a recipe, origin or restaurant tradition.

**Mark and type:** Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold gives the named lockup an assured, slightly irregular voice. An asymmetric V-like fold adds a compact signature: the right wing rises, and an open notch prevents a closed badge. The source symbol uses a 96 × 96 viewBox; the geometry is retained in the editable SVG. The wordmark can be stacked as in sheet 01 or set horizontally when there is room. Restaurants is never dropped in line identification. This is a typeset starting point; final spacing, optical small-size variants and exclusivity are not settled.

**Graphic language:** off-white space, dark green ink, a sharp but restrained lime plane and a quiet lavender counterplane. Folds are few and purposeful, opening a margin or separating an expressive moment. No shadows are needed to identify this line; ORILLA can retain its own material depth.

**Character:** one conceptual folded-paper host with a lifted arm, small eyes and an open gesture. The drawing is visibly graphic and separate from the mark. It suggests an invitation; it is not an employee, chef, ingredient, factual assistant or definitive mascot. The line remains identifiable through full name/type/shape when the face is absent.

**Strength:** the personality can live in line material and retreat on client consultation. A photo-free specimen remains intentionally composed. **Weakness:** a V and paper fold are not intrinsically exclusive; distinctive recognition still needs comparison with existing marks before production/commercial use. The character may read as an envelope or origami object, so G2.08 must refine the fold gesture without accumulating props.

**Maintenance:** a limited reusable gesture vocabulary can avoid drawing every new dish. Each gesture, local adaptation and motion still creates asset/review work. No budget, delivery duration or low-cost promise is inferred.

### B / Sobremesa tipográfica

**Meaning:** an offer worth reading and discussing. The line is a lively editorial host rather than a folded object.

**Mark and type:** Fraunces Black, with pronounced serif shapes and a round counterform symbol. The sample character is an “o”-like letter body; it does not alter the readable product name. Names and essential text remain actual text.

**Graphic language:** warm butter field, paprika lettering, coral and deep aubergine accents; curved interruptions and editorial rhythm. Character grows from letter shape/counterspace, not the fold. Proposed line copy: **“Una carta con conversación.”**

**Strength:** expressive without photos; a richer literary/tactile personality. **Weakness:** the dense serif and restaurant-name variability need more optical setting. A typographic character can compete with local wordmarks, and the tone risks nostalgic café specificity instead of a broad restaurant line. The round symbol alone has weak attribution specificity.

**Maintenance:** editorial name fitting, character-letter drawings and language-specific compositions demand additional art-direction review. Arbitrary per-word letter mascots would turn routine copy changes into asset production; avoid that dependency.

### C / Compás de sala

**Meaning:** graphic orientation and the cadence of service.

**Mark and type:** Space Grotesk Bold, uppercase line lockup, angular V/dot symbol. Band alignment, trimmed edges and directional shapes form the rhythm. The conceptual ticket-shaped character tests the service metaphor. Proposed copy: **“Buen ritmo. Carta clara.”**

**Graphic language:** bright cool field, cobalt, navy and a limited coral accent; crisp alignment and interrupted bands.

**Strength:** a robust orderly voice that can change rhythm without requiring imagery. **Weakness:** ticket edges and arrows can imply bills, orders or transactional tools that this module does not provide. It has less hospitable warmth and can become generic wayfinding. A decorative arrow must never resemble an actionable control.

**Maintenance:** repeated band rules are reusable; bespoke signs and output-specific placement still need review. It would require stronger content safeguards against accidental transaction promises. That additional interpretation risk is why A is preferred.

### Why A wins this decision

B places more personality in letter treatment; C places it in a signalling grammar. A puts personality in an **optional hosting gesture**, keeping a strong but readable line name. That makes restaurant-led coexistence and the distinction between presentation and consultation easier to express in the bounded samples. This is an author judgement, not a scored user study. Choose B if the founder wants editorial lettering to be the main long-term investment; choose C if he consciously prefers a service-sign language and accepts its ambiguity. Do not combine all three into one palette or character family.

## 6. Character, resources, imagery and motion briefs

### G2.08 / resources — after selection

Produce a bounded character study for A, not a family for every dish. Start with the supplied fold geometry and compare an invitation gesture, a calm resting pose and a version with no face. Keep a recognisable oblique fold, limited planes, restrained line weight and enough negative space for a small reproduction. Avoid hats, stereotyped chefs, national/cuisine costumes, tongues, exaggerated food appetites and medical/safety gestures. Test whether it reads as hospitable paper rather than mail or a generic software assistant.

The current drawing is conceptual vector work made during this assignment. It establishes no finished repertoire, production licence grant, legal exclusivity or commercial clearance. Variation can use orientation, fold angle and gesture; it should not require character redesign for a new price, language or menu item. Never give a character the sole job of communicating error, sold-out, food facts, permission, uncertain result or private account state.

**Where it helps:** line introduction, a studio handover cover or a voluntary editorial hospitality moment. **Where it gets in the way:** dense consultation, direct menu access, publication/recovery, sensitive food information and already expressive restaurant imagery. Characters can be absent. On a client page any local or line character is an explicitly reviewed choice; no mandatory mascot in headers or beside amounts.

Photography should describe the actual venue and supplied food: inspectable portions, truthful crops, natural color and coherent light. Retain rights/source/usage approval with each asset. No photo is required for every item, and scarce resources should lead to a deliberate type-led composition rather than fake blanks or invented dishes. Illustrations can add mood but must not assert ingredients, recipes, provenance or portion. This package contains no newly generated food image; the preserved ORILLA reference contains previously generated conceptual food and remains labelled as such.

Functional icons must keep a separate utilitarian vocabulary, named meaning and text alternatives. The concept symbols/characters are not an allergen icon set or functional control set. G2.08 owns actual asset formats/variants and a production-effort assessment; G1 handles consequential resource decisions. No purchase or production schedule is authorised by this brief.

### G2.04 + SEO/GEO / content — prepared questions, no contribution received

Use the same identified copy version for voice and discovery review. Test whether “Cada local tiene su sitio” communicates the studio’s role without implying a booking/marketplace service. Draft a factual supporting sentence that distinguishes base design, bespoke design and restaurant maintenance. The line can sound welcoming and direct; food/price/error language must remain literal and specific. No joke is required to carry an essential instruction.

Separate the studio’s offer/name from each restaurant’s public facts and culinary vocabulary. Preserve actual restaurant names as readable text, not only a logo image. Ask SEO/GEO which additional descriptive content is needed to distinguish the studio line from restaurant discovery on this version; no ranking, citation, traffic or generative-search outcome is promised. These are review questions, not SEO rules already verified.

### G2.07 / UI and G2.03 / behaviour — boundaries

After selection, apply R1 to identified IA/copy and the preserved journeys. Compare expressive presentation with clear consultation using the same local identity. Keep direct menu entry, anonymous browsing, price/format/context, known/pending food information and registered/private account boundaries. Do not convert folded planes into mandatory cards, force a character into every state, or rasterise maintained facts. UI defines actual surfaces and responsive rules; G2.03 preserves behaviour. This package provides no Home, navigation, menu system, components or connected journey.

### G2.09 / motion and G2.10 / system — later bounded work

A fold may offer a brief invitation in an optional presentation moment if G2.09 can demonstrate a useful purpose. The static version must already contain the identity and entry. Do not prescribe autoplay, compulsory entrance animation or animated data. Compare interruption and a reduced-motion/static alternative before acceptance; no motion timings/easing/performance result are approved here. G2.10 consolidates type/color/variants only from tested applications; these specimen values are not a token library.
