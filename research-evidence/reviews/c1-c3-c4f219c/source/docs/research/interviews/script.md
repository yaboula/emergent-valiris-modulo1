# Behaviour-based owner interview guide

Prepared: 2026-09-28. Method reference: [The Mom Test, Rob Fitzpatrick](https://www.momtestbook.com/). The questions below are original adaptations for this project, not excerpts from the book.

## Purpose and use

Understand recent behaviour, constraints and existing workarounds before presenting a solution. A useful conversation describes an actual incident, what happened next and what the operator already invested in solving it. Compliments, speculative feature requests and promises to use a future product do not validate demand.

Plan 25–35 minutes. Explain the research purpose, ask permission to take minimal notes, and obtain separate permission for any recording. Do not copy customer names, booking records, health notes or contact lists. Offer anonymised descriptions or dummy examples. Never claim a fictional session is a completed interview.

## Questions

1. Walk me through your most recent busy service. Who handled phone, walk-in and online reservations?
2. Tell me about the last reservation that changed or was missed. Where was the change recorded, and who saw it?
3. When did a guest last fail to arrive without cancelling? What did you do with the table, and what costs actually remained?
4. Show me, using dummy data, how you check capacity and change a party size. What happens when two staff members update it?
5. When did you last change a dish, price, translation or availability? Who made the change, and how did you verify every published version?
6. How did you answer the last allergen question? Where did the answer come from, and how do you handle an uncertain recipe or variant?
7. Tell me about the last time you recognised a returning guest. What information did you have, and what did you do with it?
8. How does your current loyalty process work when a card is missing, a phone changes or a stamp is disputed?
9. Describe the most recent review request or public reply. Who decided whom to ask? Was anything offered in return?
10. When did you last contact a guest who had stopped visiting? How was permission collected, and what happened after sending?
11. What was the last question you answered repeatedly over phone or WhatsApp? How much service time did it interrupt?
12. What software or manual tools did you pay for last month? Which contract, staff limit or integration mattered when choosing them?
13. Tell me about your last tool change or support incident. What would make switching impractical, even if a new product looked useful?

Follow up with: when, who, what happened next, how often in a defined period, and what evidence could be shown safely. Distinguish observed facts, estimates and interpretation. Ask about an actual past purchase rather than a hypothetical price for Valiris.

## Close and record

Summarise the incident back to the participant and invite correction. Ask whether an additional conversation or a short task walkthrough would be useful; do not treat agreement as a purchase commitment. Record segment, date, incident, workaround, impact, contrary evidence and unanswered questions. Do not retain identifying details without a research need and appropriate information/permission.

## Simulation protocol

The three accompanying documents are **fictional scenario exercises**, written on 2026-09-28. All venue characteristics, incidents and responses are invented. They explore how this guide might expose requirements or disprove a hypothesis; they contribute zero real participants and zero independent demand evidence. Public sources referenced by a scenario support the question's relevance, not the fictional answer.
