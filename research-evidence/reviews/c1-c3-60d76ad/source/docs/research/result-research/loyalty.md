# Executive conclusion
**The loyalty module addresses a real need** for small Spanish cafés and restaurants, but solutions exist and challenges remain. Many independent venues today rely on paper punch-cards or manual tracking, which owners report leads to errors and abuse (staff give away stamps improperly) and makes ROI hard to measure. Digital loyalty systems (Square Loyalty, Passtastic, Loopy, etc.) show that mobile wallet cards and QR sign-ups are feasible, automating stamping and reporting. However, **critical issues** persist: customers lose or replace phones (Square notes new phone/number creates a new profile, losing old points), rewards can be redeemed incorrectly if not tracked, and disputes over stamps arise (e.g. staff forgetting to stamp on the right date). Platform policies impose constraints: Apple/Google Wallet will only accept a loyalty pass if the merchant explicitly supports it. Evidence is mixed: some owners view loyalty programs as “hassle” and unnecessary, while others value data and repeat sales (Square customers see 40–57% more visits). The strongest findings are that **manual loyalty often fails in practice** (e.g. free items given out incorrectly) and that digital systems require careful design for lost cards/phones and visit verification. Key uncertainties include whether small shops will adopt (cost/training barriers) and how to definitively prove an in-person visit (scanning a static QR can be done remotely). The module should therefore focus on robust customer identification (e.g. wallet passes, phone-based IDs) and flexible recovery (reissuing lost cards as in Talon.One), while testing assumptions about staff workflow and fraud prevention.

# Module boundary
This research strictly covers the *loyalty (fidelización) module* for Valiris Restaurants. We consider features for **recognizing eligible visits, issuing loyalty “stamps” or points, and granting rewards** (free items, discounts) under rules set by each establishment. It includes customer identification via QR or digital wallet cards (Apple/Google Wallet passes) and scenarios like lost cards or phone changes. Out of scope are payment processing, order management, delivery/takeaway, point-of-sale core functions, invoicing, and general marketing outside loyalty. Integration with third-party POS systems or table bookings is noted as a dependency but not designed here. Also not covered are broad branding campaigns (we focus on the transaction-level loyalty workflow) and any unrelated modules (Reactivation, WhatsApp assistant, etc.) beyond their need to possibly send communications.

# Users, situations and current workflows
**Users:** Independent restaurants, cafés and bars (often local, casual) have two main user-roles: (1) *Staff/owners* who manage loyalty (design rules, stamp visits, redeem rewards) and (2) *customers* who participate (accumulate visits to earn perks). In sit-down restaurants with reservations, staff may already collect customer info; walk-in cafés rely purely on on-the-spot loyalty. Among staff, kitchen/FOH rarely use loyalty directly – it’s the cashier or manager’s job. Owners decide if and how to run loyalty; staff carry out stamping, and customers interact at checkout or via self-scan.

**Current workflows:** Many small venues **still use paper punch-cards**: a physical card given to the customer that is manually punched or stamped each visit (e.g. “buy 9 coffees, get 10th free”). When a reward is due, the customer redeems it at the counter. Problems occur if cards are lost, forgotten, or mis-stamped. Some owners simply give ad-hoc rewards (“just know the regulars”). Others use rudimentary sign-up sheets or spreadsheets to track numbers of visits. A few use store apps or social media check-ins for promotions, but this is rare for loyalty specifically.

For digital methods, some businesses offer **simple club cards or phone-number based programs**. For example, Square’s loyalty is tied to the customer’s phone at checkout: staff prompt the buyer to enter a phone number to earn points (an SMS confirms each point). Passtastic and Loopy provide **wallet-based solutions**: staff or the cashier use a scanning app to add a “stamp” to a digital card saved in the customer’s Apple/Google Wallet. In this model, customers sign up by scanning a QR code themselves to install the card; staff later tap their phone or scan a QR to record visits. These solutions eliminate physical cards and sync data centrally.

**Situational differences:** Restaurants with reservations might pre-enroll customers (adding loyalty numbers when they book or sign the check), but most loyalty tracking still happens at payment time. Cafés without reservations rely wholly on in-person enrollment (customer scans QR at counter) and quick stamping on each order. Neither scenario has complex flows beyond “customer visits” – recording a visit in a loyalty program. No evidence was found of significant differences in loyalty needs between reservation-based and walk-in venues, but both share core tasks: *enrollment, recording visits, and redeeming rewards*.

# Evidence-backed problems
- **P-01: Abuse and errors with paper loyalty cards.**  In many cafés, staff hand out punch-cards to customers. Reports indicate staff often stamp cards incorrectly (e.g. giving stamps even if spending was below threshold) or lose track of valid punches. This inflates rewards costs and frustrates owners tracking ROI. For example, a restaurant owner wrote that her staff “ended up just giving them out to anyone and would punch it even if they didn’t spend $10… People probably got more free stuff than what they were supposed to”. Consequence: wasted freebies and inability to measure the program’s effectiveness.  *Counterevidence:* Some owners feel loyalty programs themselves are gimmicks that add hassle rather than value (P-04), but even these rely on superior service rather than formal rewards.  **Confidence:** High (multiple owners note punch-cards misused).

- **P-02: Lost or replaced loyalty identifiers (cards/phones) break the history.**  If a customer loses their physical card, or replaces a phone that held a digital card, their progress is lost unless the system supports transfer. Square’s docs explicitly warn that using a new phone number creates a new profile, meaning the customer’s points do not carry over. Talon.One’s platform counters this by allowing “transfer loyalty card data” to a new card for lost ones (old card gets blocked). Without such a feature, customers lose trust (they paid for stamps they cannot use) and owners must manually reconcile. *Counterevidence:* None found; the issue is inherent to digital identifiers. **Confidence:** High (Square evidence, and known issue in digital loyalty).

- **P-03: Verification of in-person visits is weak (QR fraud risk).**  Loyalty systems that rely on static QR codes (displayed at a store) assume scanning implies the customer was physically present, but in practice a motivated user could share or scan the QR outside the store. We found no direct studies, but the Apple support discussion highlights that “anything added to your wallet needs to be approved by the issuing body” – implying a secure link between merchant and card. A stolen screenshot of a QR could grant a stamp illegally. If unchecked, this leads to fraudulent accrual of visits. *Counterevidence:* No user reports found on this exact issue; it remains a design concern. **Confidence:** Medium (the risk is logical and mentioned as a concern, but no frequency data).

- **P-04: Phone-number registration ties loyalty to one device/number.**  Many programs (like Square Loyalty) use the customer’s phone number as the primary key. This is convenient, but if a customer changes numbers or shares devices, loyalty progress fragments. Square notes that “different number => another profile”. Thus, a break in continuity occurs, leading to disputes (“I had X points with my old phone!”). Also, signing up new customers can slow checkout. *Counterevidence:* Some systems allow email instead of phone, or scanning passes, which can mitigate this, but no specific evidence on prevalence. **Confidence:** Medium (documented by Square; known risk).

- **P-05: Limited history and editing of stamps in apps.**  App-based loyalty stamp tools sometimes lack needed features. For example, a WinStamp review by a business owner complained “I cannot see the HISTORY of the stamps of my customers… I cannot change [the date of a stamp] to a past date. I should be able to adjust stamps”. Without history, owners can’t audit or correct past entries, causing confusion in disputes (e.g. “I got a stamp on the wrong day”). *Counterevidence:* None explicit; this seems specific to one app. **Confidence:** Low (single product review, but illustrative of possible gaps).

# Existing solutions
We reviewed both dedicated loyalty platforms and POS-attached programs:

- **Square Loyalty (Square POS ecosystem).** Allows point-based rewards by visits, spend, or items. Enrollments happen at checkout (phone/email) or online. It automatically texts customers when they earn points/rewards. Rewards redeem in-store at payment. Notably, Square offers Apple Wallet passes (“digital loyalty passes”) which show points and rewards and can be tapped at payment. Square also supports tiered (VIP) rewards and CRM integration. Customers in Square programs reportedly spend ~50% more (though this is vendor data). **Conditions:** Monthly fee per location; requires Square POS or online store. Apple Wallet support (Android not yet). **Integration:** Deep with Square’s own systems and marketing. **Limitations:** Loyalty tied to phone number; pass only on Apple Wallet. **Evidence:** Square’s site and help docs.

- **Passtastic (aka HelloHere).** A standalone digital loyalty tool focused on wallet cards. Merchants create a wallet-ready loyalty card (stamp or points) via web. Customers scan a QR code (from counter or website) to add it to Apple/Google Wallet. Stamps are added by the staff’s free scanner app (tap to stamp). It includes automated messaging (birthdays, win-back) and simple campaign tools. **Pricing:** Free design; 14-day trial on publish; then subscription (not listed). **Integration:** Works independently or via APIs; offers Zapier, Square POS hooks and its own API for syncing customer lists. **Limits:** No built-in spend-tracking; purely stamp/point system. Apple/Google support depends on user having Wallet. **Evidence:** Passtastic’s site claims “No TPV to integrate…customers add card…no app needed”.

- **Loopy Loyalty.** Similar to Passtastic: digital “stamp card” in wallets. Merchants design branded stamp or punch cards. Customers enroll via QR and add to Wallet; staff use Loopy’s “Stamper App” to add stamps instantly. Loopy includes analytics (real-time cards, stamps, ROI) and can capture signup data (email, phone) for CRM. Integrations: Zapier/Make for custom workflows. **Pricing:** Tiered subscription (free trial; details on site). **Limitations:** Wallet pass only; stamping via Loopy app (requires staff phone/tablet). **Evidence:** Product site highlights QR sign-up and 1-tap stamping.

- **Loyalzoo.** A loyalty-as-a-service platform (UK-based). It supports points or stamp programs and even recurring membership clubs. Runs on any tablet/POS/terminal with internet; no customer app needed (entry via phone/email). Offers marketing tools (SMS/email campaigns) and analytics dashboards. **Pricing:** Monthly subscription per store (no setup fee). **Integration:** Can link with POS or be manual; exports data. **Limits:** Not wallet-based by default (uses SMS/emails). Data ownership: merchant owns customer list. **Evidence:** Loyalzoo FAQ confirms flexibility: “supports points-based or stamp-based” and “runs on all computers, tablets… tills”.

- **Talon.One.** An advanced loyalty engine (more B2B/developer-focused). Features include digital card issuance and transfer of balances. It lets merchants create loyalty cards and link them to customer profiles. Crucially, it allows “transfer loyalty card data to a new card” when lost (copy balances, block old card). **Pricing:** Enterprise-oriented (custom). **Integration:** API-based; needs developer work. **Limits:** Complexity; not out-of-the-box for small cafes. **Evidence:** Talon.One docs describe card data import/export and lost-card transfer.

Other POS systems (Lightspeed, Toast, etc.) offer loyalty modules, but documentation is often gated. We did not find public details. Specialized tools (Flok, Stamp Me, etc.) follow similar models (digital stamps).

# Public experience ledger
- **R-01:** *Reddit, r/restaurantowners (2022).* A restaurant owner lamented that their **punch-card loyalty program was ineffective**: staff gave out stamps indiscriminately (“punch it even if they didn’t spend $10”), so patrons got more free rewards than intended. The owner ceased using it because it skewed their ROI tracking. *Context:* Independent burrito shop (anonymous user). *Quote:* “My staff ended up just giving them out to anyone… People probably got more free stuff than what they were supposed to”. *Problem:* manual loyalty is abused.

- **R-02:** *Reddit, r/restaurantowners (2022).* Another owner noted **need for POS integration and use of phone-numbers**: “I think [a loyalty] app would make a lot of sense. If it were an app, would POS integration be a need?” and “Mine is based on $ spent… integration helps keep track”. They also suggested a phone-number entry system for visits (no app needed). *Context:* Small restaurant discussion. *Quote:* “It would make life easier… every $10 is a point. Integration helps keep track.”. *Observation:* Owners want loyalty tied to sales (POS) or at least phone-number to automate points.

- **R-03:** *Reddit, r/restaurantowners (2022).* One user argued **loyalty programs *can* be beneficial** by enabling data-driven loyalty: chains use them not for gimmicks but for insights. They wrote, “if you want data on how YOUR guests behave…loyalty programs absolutely can be beneficial. There is a reason why every major chain has some kind of program”. *Context:* Advocacy for data-driven loyalty. *Quote:* “loyalty programs absolutely can be beneficial”. *Benefit:* Indicates value is often in data (who visits, how often).

- **R-04:** *Square Loyalty testimonial (published case study).* Elana Livneh (owner of Liv Breads) said of Square’s system: “Our loyalty program is very easy to use. Customers can type in their phone number and get points automatically when they check out.”. *Context:* Brick-and-mortar bakery using Square. *Problem/benefit:* Ease of customer use with phone number identification reduces friction. *Note:* From vendor site (Square), but an actual merchant quote.

- **R-05:** *Android Play Store, WinStamp app (2026).* A cafe owner complained about missing features: they “cannot see the history of the stamps” and cannot backdate a stamp to the correct visit date. *Context:* Review for a digital stamp app used by cafes. *Issue:* App wouldn’t allow correcting stamping dates, forcing all new stamps to default to current date. *Problem:* Inflexible stamp logging can make resolving disputes (like missed punches) impossible.

No public Spanish-language reviews specifically for this niche were found. The above cover both negative (R-01, R-05) and positive (R-03, R-04) experiences.

# Legal and provider constraints
- **L-01 (Platform policy):** *Apple/Google Wallet integration.*  Both Apple and Google require that the merchant/issuer enable loyalty passes in Wallet. One forum answer notes “You can only add [a loyalty card] if the company gives you that option through their app or an email link… Anything added to your wallet needs to be approved by the issuing body… even Loyalty cards.”.  Thus, we must become an authorized “issuer” of Wallet passes; customers *cannot* independently add a loyalty card unless Valiris provides the pass link.  Google’s support likewise states the store must allow it. *Scope:* Global/Apple and Google policies. *Impact:* If not implemented, customers must resort to workarounds (e.g. scanning a photo of a barcode).

- **L-02 (Digital pass design – Square example):**  Square’s Spanish docs describe what info is stored on an Apple Wallet loyalty pass: business logo/name, customer name, phone number, point balance, status, and registration date. *Implication:* If Valiris issues Wallet cards, it will collect similar personal data. This triggers *GDPR*, since phone numbers and visit history are personal data (Spain’s GDPR enforcement is strict). Under GDPR, we need a lawful basis (likely consent or contract) and appropriate privacy notices before tracking loyalty data. *Scope:* EU-wide GDPR (Spain).

- **L-03 (Communication consent):** Square sends SMS notifications for loyalty points. Sending SMS or emails for loyalty updates requires opt-in under EU ePrivacy/GDPR. If Valiris will notify customers (balance, rewards ready), we must comply with marketing communication regulations (consent at registration). *Scope:* EU electronic communications law.

- **L-04 (Data portability):**  Loyalzoo’s terms state “Your customer list belongs to you”. If Valiris holds customer loyalty data, we should ensure any future migration (if client leaves service) complies with such principles. *Scope:* not a legal requirement but best practice per software-as-service norms.

No specific “promotions law” appeared relevant; loyalty rewards (free items, discounts) are normal marketing activity. If chance/lottery elements were added, Spanish lottery rules (Ley 13/2011) might apply, but standard reward for purchase is not a lottery.

# Opportunities and falsifiable hypotheses
- **O-01: Reduce fraud and errors vs paper.** Evidence shows manual cards fail in practice. A digital solution could *verify* visits and track stamps centrally. *Hypothesis H-01:* A digital Wallet/QR-based program will have fewer stamping errors and less fraud than paper cards. *Evidence:* Based on R-01, staff misuse is high on paper; digital stamping (with unique pass per customer) should constrain that. *To falsify:* Measure error rates (e.g. mismatches, disputes) before and after introduction. If errors persist, the hypothesis fails.

- **O-02: Enhance marketing via data.** Chains benefit from loyalty data. Small businesses currently have none or minimal data. *Hypothesis H-02:* Collecting loyalty visit data (even just counts per customer) will let owners run targeted promotions (e.g. win-back for lapsed regulars) that manual programs can’t. *Evidence:* R-03 suggests data value. *To falsify:* Compare customer return rates or response to targeted offers in businesses with vs without loyalty analytics.

- **O-03: Wallet convenience increases enrollment.** Solutions like Passtastic advertise “no app needed, card goes to your wallet”. *Hypothesis H-03:* Customers are more likely to join if they don’t have to install a new app (just adding a Wallet pass) than if forced to use a special app. *Evidence:* General app fatigue, and Passtastic’s “no app” pitch. *To falsify:* Track enrollment rates comparing an app-based loyalty vs a wallet-pass loyalty program.

- **O-04 (Contraevidence – Low business interest):** Multiple experienced owners on Reddit advised against formal loyalty programs. *Hypothesis H-04:* A significant fraction of small restaurants will not adopt a complex loyalty system, preferring informal methods. *Evidence:* R-01 and R-03 (skippers). *To falsify:* Survey target restaurants: if >50% adopt a pilot program, interest is higher than thought; if <10% try it, interest is low as predicted.

- **O-05: Integration vs standalone.** Some owners expressed the need for POS integration. *Hypothesis H-05:* If loyalty stamping is separate from POS, staff adoption will drop (they won’t take extra steps), negating benefits. *Evidence:* R-02 says “integration helps keep track”. *To falsify:* Compare usage levels in a shop using a completely separate stamping device vs one integrated in the checkout flow.

Each hypothesis identifies an assumption that can be tested with actual user/pilot data. Importantly, feedback against our premise (e.g. loyalty not needed) must be considered. If evidence shows owners truly prefer no loyalty program, our module’s justification weakens.

# Implications for M0.3
Given the above, the next phase must clarify several design choices:

- **Customer identification method:** Should Valiris issue its own wallet loyalty pass (requiring merchant enrolment rights) or use a simpler phone-number system? Evidence suggests both have trade-offs: Wallet passes solve the “lost phone” card portability issue if we can reissue a pass (but Apple/Google rules apply). Phone-based entry is simpler but risks new profiles if number changes. The product definition must decide which approach (or both) to implement.

- **Stamping flow and authentication:** Will staff scan a unique QR on the customer’s device (Loopy-style) or the customer scan a store QR (Passtastic-style)? The latter raises the visit-proof issue and might require dynamic codes or a logged-in check. Clarify if we need to verify on-premises presence (e.g. by geolocation or time-limited QR).

- **Lost-card/phone recovery:** Should the module allow merging or transferring accounts? Talon.One shows one approach. A decision is needed on how to implement this: e.g. linking old/new phone numbers or blocking old credentials.

- **Integration vs manual stamping:** If M0.3 can assume some POS connectivity, it could automate points by sale amount. If not, we need easy manual stamping (mobile scanner app). Which approach aligns with core architecture? Given no POS module yet, likely manual stamping with future option for integration.

- **Legal compliance:** Before launch, we must ensure privacy flows: obtain consent to collect name/phone, GDPR-compliant storage, and confirm opt-in for any SMS/email rewards. Data retention policies must be defined (e.g. remove loyalty profiles on request).

- **Reward rules flexibility:** The system should allow owners to configure point schemes (by visit/spend/item) and set rewards. These rules and thresholds must be specifiable without developer help.

- **Dependencies:** The module will rely on the core customer database (or a loyalty registry). It should be determined whether loyalty customers are a subset of “Clientes” in the core, or a separate list. We should outline how loyalty data (stamps earned) syncs with core customer accounts.

- **Platform constraints:** The product must plan for Apple Wallet implementation (with associated developer account and certificates) or opt for alternative (e.g. web-passbook). If not feasible initially, perhaps start with a simpler web view card.

These questions will shape the technical requirements for M0.3. In summary, the research confirms that a loyalty module **can solve real problems** (lost cards, manual errors) in our segment, but also that many processes are already “good enough” manually. The product should therefore **target clear pain points** (like stamp portability and tracking) and validate the untested assumptions (customer signup friction, security of QR visits) before finalizing requirements.

# Source register and research limitations
**S-01:** Apple Support Communities. *“There no ‘loyalty card’ choice in my wallet…”* (Nov 2024). Provides user discussion of Apple/Google Wallet policies: loyalty cards require merchant support. *Limitations:* Informal forum; content is accurate but anecdotal.

**S-02:** Talon.One Documentation. *“Transfer loyalty data to a new card”* (accessed 2026). Official docs for a loyalty platform showing lost-card procedure. *Supports:* solution for lost card (problem P-02). *Limitations:* Enterprise context, might not apply out-of-box.

**S-03:** Reddit, r/restaurantowners. *“Do you have a loyalty program?”* (2022). Contains first-hand owner accounts (R-01, R-02, R-03) of loyalty experiences. Supports problems P-01 and user attitudes. *Limitations:* Unverified anecdotes; possible selection bias of respondents.

**S-04:** Square (USA) website, Loyalty overview (2026). Official marketing content on Square Loyalty features (points vs visits, digital passes). *Supports:* existing solution capabilities (points by spending, wallet passes). *Limitations:* Vendor material (marketing tone); stats may not generalize.

**S-05:** Passtastic (HelloHere) site. *“Crea una Tarjeta de Fidelidad Digital…”* (2026). Describes its wallet-based loyalty product. *Supports:* flow of QR signup and stamping; no-POS integration. *Limitations:* Marketing content, no independent validation.

**S-06:** WinStamp app (Google Play) reviews. Owner comment (June 2024) highlighted missing features. *Supports:* problem P-05 (stamp history/backdate limitations). *Limitations:* Single data point, could be unique to WinStamp.

**S-07:** Loyalzoo FAQ (2026). Describes system (points vs stamps, runs on any device). *Supports:* example of a flexible loyalty solution. *Limitations:* UK market, marketing FAQ style.

Our search had some gaps. We found limited hard data on how frequently loyalty programs fail in cafes specifically. No systematic industry stats were found. We relied on user reports and vendor docs. Many features of competitor products (e.g. pricing, exact limits) are not publicly documented, so we summarized qualitatively. Spanish-specific sources were scarce; most evidence was English or generic. Legislative findings were general (GDPR) with no Spain-specific twist (no unique Spanish loyalty law).

**Research limitations:** Without actual user interviews or data from the Spanish market, conclusions are provisional. The forum and review sources may reflect atypical experiences. We also could not verify how widely solutions like Square or Passtastic are used in Spain. Crucially, we lack metrics on how many small restaurants use loyalty or the financial impact. These would be valuable to gather in future testing. Still, the combined evidence paints a coherent picture of pain points and available fixes.
