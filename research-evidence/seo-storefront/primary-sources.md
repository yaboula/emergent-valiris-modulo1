# Primary sources — ORILLA SEO/GEO review

Consulted **4 October 2026**, using the web tool to read official documentation. This is a new verification for this review, not a relabelling of the 3 October historical preparation. Short excerpts below record the pertinent statement; linked pages remain the authority. No full external article is reproduced. Platform rules do not establish that an unimplemented ORILLA site satisfies them.

### S01

**Google Search Central — [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).**

Excerpt: “There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary.”

Platform statement: ordinary search eligibility/SEO guidance applies; no AI-specific file or special schema is necessary. Important content should be available as text; linked eligibility requires indexing and snippet eligibility and does not guarantee appearance.

Application/inference: review clear linked blocks and text rather than commission another AI page. This does not select Googlebot/access policies or prove ORILLA indexing, citation or ranking. Review links: R09/R10/R12; Google training uses require a separate policy discussion.

### S02

**OpenAI — [Overview of OpenAI Crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).**

Excerpt: “Each setting is independent of the others”.

Platform statement: OAI-SearchBot supports search; GPTBot concerns model training; ChatGPT-User is user-triggered retrieval and does not determine search inclusion. User-initiated actions are distinct from automatic crawling, and robots rules may not apply to those requests.

Application: ask G1/G3 for separate access policies and verification, not a blanket recommendation to permit GPTBot for discovery. No access configuration, IP verification or bot visit has been tested. Review link: R12/T06.

### S03

**Google Search Central — [General structured data guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies).**

Excerpt: “Don't mark up irrelevant or misleading content, such as fake reviews or content unrelated to the focus of a page.”

Platform statement: markup must represent relevant visible content and meet feature-specific rules; valid syntax does not guarantee a rich result.

Application/inference: ORILLA's authorised fiction and unverified food examples cannot become purported real-business reviews or verified dietary facts. The contract supplies that fiction boundary; Google documentation is not quoted as a specific prohibition of all demonstration markup. Review link: R11/T07.

### S04

**Google Search Central — [How to specify a canonical URL](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls).**

Excerpt: “Don't specify a URL fragment as canonical”.

Platform statement: fragment identifiers are not canonical URL targets; multilingual canonical relationships need language-aware handling, including a same-language canonical where possible.

Application/inference: A's Home fragments support reading/navigation, not a claim of separate canonical pages. G3 must decide duplicates and locale handling from actual delivery. No canonical element or route is configured. Review links: R08/R09/T01/T03.

### S05

**Google Search Central — [Managing multi-regional and multilingual sites](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites).**

Excerpt: “Google recommends using different URLs for each language version of a page”.

Platform statement: visible content determines language; language-specific URLs are recommended, and language accessibility should not rely on automatic redirection.

Application/inference: an English interface around Spanish dish facts is not a reviewed English content version. Keep fallback meaning visible; G3/G2.05 decide actual URLs/canonical/hreflang and translation coverage. No translations or locale implementation tested. Review link: R08/T03.

### S06

**Google Search Central — [Make your links crawlable](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable).**

Excerpt: “Generally, Google can only crawl your link if it's an <a> HTML element”.

Platform statement: a resolvable `href` and meaningful anchor text support discovery. Correctly formed links inserted through JavaScript can qualify; this source does not justify a blanket claim that every AI crawler cannot render JavaScript.

Application: specify descriptive story/item/task destinations for later delivery. No HTML/rendered link audit has taken place. Review links: R02/R03/R04/R09; technical link delivery goes to T05.

### S07

**Google Search Central — [Local business structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business).**

Excerpt: “The physical location of the business.”

Platform statement: Google's LocalBusiness feature requires its applicable fields, including a name and physical address. Restaurant properties and opening-hour fields describe real supplied business facts.

Application/inference: a city setting without premises is not a verified physical address. Do not borrow another establishment's location, create a real listing or claim this fiction meets the feature requirements. The authored local/kitchen distinction remains necessary public content regardless of future markup. Review links: R05/R06/R11/T07.

### S08

**Schema.org — [MenuItem](https://schema.org/MenuItem).**

Excerpt: “A food or drink item listed in a menu or menu section.”

Vocabulary statement: the consulted page identifies itself as the development version, describes menu items and illustrates restaurant/menu/section/item/offer relationships. This is a vocabulary reference, not a pinned production specification, Google-supported feature list or endorsement of example facts.

Application/inference: use C's existing entity/offer IDs when preparing later fact-to-field questions. Dietary or nutrition properties are not permission to infer verified facts. No markup, validator or rich-result test was run. Review link: R11/T07.

## Method limits

The SEO, content, GEO, schema, local, plan, cluster and SXO skills were applied within the contract. Their generic scoring, word-count/density heuristics and unsupported platform assumptions were not adopted as requirements. Task/entity relationships here derive from the shared case and approved functional sources; they are not SERP-validated clusters or observed demand. No competitor benchmark, live-site crawl, Google Places listing, account metric or tool configuration was required or performed.

Official rules above are labelled separately from case observations and editorial inference. Their consultation does not resolve G1/G3 policies or replace UI, linguistic, technical or independent review.
