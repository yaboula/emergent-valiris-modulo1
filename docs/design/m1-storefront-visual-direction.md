### Recognizable language and adaptation

**Personality:** a contemporary Mediterranean table, generous in expression and precise in information. The restaurant should be recognizable before the visitor reads a story; its name, culinary proposition and direct menu entry carry the first encounter. These are direction principles, not verified brand or commercial outcomes.

The recognizable combination is a name with graphic weight, culinary imagery sharing the same compositional field, luminous mint/raspberry planes and dark readable type. Food is the subject, not wallpaper; contact shadows give objects material presence without enclosing the experience in decorative cards. This expresses the restaurant, not Studio Valiris.

Keep that relationship rather than every coordinate. UI may vary scale, alignment, section count, spacing and image placement for content and viewport; architecture/content decide the sequence. Do not force a long name into ORILLA's single-line proportions, require a photograph for every item, or add unsupported restaurant stories to fill a layout. Mint/raspberry are the selected ORILLA reference, not an obligatory palette for every cuisine. Exact color tokens and accessible combinations remain UI/design-system work; the raster does not specify them.

Presentation can be expressive; consultation must let names, format, amount, context and declared/pending information lead. Identity should survive the reduction of image size and display type. Keep the separate Digital Menu and immediate **Ver carta** entry; this guide does not prescribe its layout or postpone accepted capabilities.
