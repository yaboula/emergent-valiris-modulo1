# Spanish restaurant market

Research date: 2026-09-28. This desk research supports a portfolio product; it does not establish a sales forecast.

## Segment and market boundary

The working segment is independent neighbourhood and casual restaurants and cafes in Spain with repeat local guests. The operator needs a practical menu, direct reservations where relevant, and a manageable relationship with returning guests. A walk-in cafe and a restaurant with booked dinner shifts have different operational needs; booking volume must not be assumed for every venue.

The INE's 2024 Structural Business Statistics report assigns **9.0% of service-sector turnover to food and beverage service activities**. Its broader hospitality total, EUR 117,440 million, includes accommodation and must not be presented as restaurant revenue. Neither figure measures the number of independent venues ready to buy this product. [INE, 2024 reference year, published 2026-03-31](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/EEESS2024.htm).

CNAE division 56 includes food and beverage service activities beyond the proposed segment. The 2025 classification also changes activity subdivisions. Enterprise counts, local establishment counts and hospitality employment are different measures. A commercial TAM/SAM/SOM requires a consistent classification, venue count, independence filter and tested price; those inputs have not been established. [INE, CNAE-2025](https://www.ine.es/daco/daco42/clasificaciones/cnae25/CNAE_2025.pdf).

## Digitalisation: size matters

In the INE's Q1 2025 ICT survey, **79.9% of enterprises with fewer than 10 employees had internet access**; among those connected businesses, **37.0% had a website and 37.8% used social media**. For connected enterprises with at least 10 employees, website adoption was **84.5%**. These are cross-sector figures within the survey's coverage, not restaurant-specific adoption rates. The denominators differ; the 37.0% website figure is not a share of all microenterprises. [INE, published 2025-10-22](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ETICCE20241T2025.htm).

**Inference:** design for an operator who may already use a phone and social channels but cannot maintain a complex software stack. This supports investigating mobile administration and a shareable public page. It does not prove that businesses without a website want to pay for one. The verified availability of low-cost menu products and broader suites also means that digitalisation is an established category, rather than an empty market. See [competitors](competitors.md).

### Sector-specific qualitative evidence

SEGITTUR's June 2026 presentation of the HORECA digital activation plan identifies training, available time, unclear perceived return and distrust of poorly matched tools as adoption barriers. It highlights reservations/capacity, loyalty and operational efficiency alongside economic control and payment integration. The published summary describes prior research, interviews, group work and observation; it does not disclose enough sample detail to estimate national prevalence. Its tourist-HORECA population also differs from the local-repeat-guest segment. [SEGITTUR, 2026-06-18](https://semantica.segittur.es/sala-de-prensa/notas-de-prensa/segittur-y-hosteleria-de-espana-presentan-el-plan-de-activacion-digital-del-sector-horeca-turistico/index.html).

**Inference:** prioritise short setup, visible task value and comprehensible daily workflows. The cited need for payment integration is also counterevidence for a POS-independent companion: keeping duplicate records could outweigh its benefits. The full report was not extracted because the accessible download exceeded the retrieval limit; these observations rely on the primary publisher's summary, not an invented reading of the report.

## No-shows: useful evidence, limited generalisation

TheFork's August 2025 article reports that its Spanish platform's monthly no-show rates in January–July 2025 stayed below **3.4%**. Its user survey attributes non-attendance to forgetting, last-minute problems and reluctance to call. The article does not provide sufficient sampling detail to treat these responses as representative of all Spanish diners. Platform customers already receive the platform's interventions. [TheFork, 2025-08-14](https://www.theforkmanager.com/es/blog/qu%C3%A9-razones-llevan-a-los-espa%C3%B1oles-a-hacer-un-no-show-en-un-restaurante).

A separate article dated March 2025 actually cites a **2016** associated-restaurant study with a 3.6% average. Its page date does not make that estimate current. Do not combine it with the 2025 figures or repeat broad revenue-loss ranges as national facts. [TheFork, historical study reference](https://www.theforkmanager.com/es/blog/sistema-reservas-reducir-noshow).

## Economics model, not an observed result

For a defined period:

`unrecovered covers = booked covers × cover-based no-show rate × (1 − refill share)`

`foregone gross sales = unrecovered covers × average spend per cover`

`foregone contribution ≈ foregone gross sales × contribution margin`

All inputs must use compatible units. A booking-based rate cannot be substituted for a cover-based rate without party-size data. Contribution margin excludes avoidable variable costs; sunk preparation costs may need separate treatment. Deposits and penalties are separate receipts, not automatically recovered food sales.

**Illustrative assumptions only:** 1,000 booked covers/month, 3% absent covers, 20% refilled, EUR 25/cover, 65% contribution margin produce 24 unrecovered covers, EUR 600 foregone sales and EUR 390 estimated contribution. These values are fictional; the 3% assumption is not derived from TheFork's booking-based rate. They are not a product ROI claim.

## What remains unvalidated

- Actual missed-call volume, no-show impact and administrative time in the target segment.
- Who currently maintains menus, translations and allergen records, and how often changes occur.
- Willingness to replace a provider, pay for a companion tool or maintain duplicate customer data.
- Whether repeat-visit tracking adds enough value without POS integration.

Public operator reviews provide candidate failure modes, not incidence estimates. Simulated scenarios provide questions, not answers from customers. Both limitations carry into [Gate 1](synthesis.md#gate-1-review-record).
