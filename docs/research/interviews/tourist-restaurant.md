# Simulated scenario: tourist-facing restaurant

**SIMULATED — FICTIONAL.** No interview took place. Every profile detail and response below is invented; no real person, business, transaction or metric is represented. Prepared 2026-09-28 using the [interview guide](script.md).

## Profile sheet (fictional assumptions)

- Independent restaurant in central Valencia; 54 seats, seasonal tourist demand and some local regulars.
- Reservations from a marketplace, phone and website; Spanish and English printed menus.
- Owner oversees shifts; floor manager handles bookings and public replies.
- Constraint: capacity across channels must remain consistent; many tourist guests will never return.

This is a contrast case, not the recommended primary pilot segment.

## Simulated conversation

**Question:** Walk through a recent repeated guest question.

**Fictional response:** Several callers asked whether a dish contained an allergen and whether a large group could book. The manager checked the kitchen record and the diary rather than improvising an answer.

**Question:** Describe a recent cancellation or no-show.

**Fictional response:** Staff tried to refill the table with waiting guests. A deposit for a special group had been disclosed in advance; other bookings did not carry one.

**Question:** What happened when you changed a booking across channels?

**Fictional response:** The restaurant needed to check its marketplace as well as its direct diary. Accepting more direct bookings without integration could create another capacity conflict.

**Question:** How did you request the last public review?

**Fictional response:** Staff informally asked guests who seemed pleased. The owner had not considered that selecting only positive experiences could breach the review platform's policy.

**Question:** How useful was the last loyalty offer?

**Fictional response:** For tourists staying a few days, a long-term stamp card was less useful than clear menu information. Local guests might behave differently, but no measurement had been taken.

## Scenario-derived hypotheses, not findings

- Multilingual information and safe escalation may be useful, but the conversational product must not guess allergen safety or capacity.
- A standalone booking tool must account for bookings taken elsewhere; no multi-channel synchronisation is promised by this research.
- Deposits, saved cards and temporary holds need distinct states and explanations.
- Review invitation eligibility must not depend on feedback score.
- Reactivation is a weak primary proposition for mostly one-time tourist demand.

Public prompts: [TheFork's platform-specific no-show evidence](../market.md), [channel and payment constraints](../legal.md), [incumbent booking operations](../competitors.md). These sources do not substantiate the fictional responses.

## What a real conversation must test

Ask about actual demand sources, repeat share, booking-channel reconciliation and written cancellation conditions. Determine whether a companion tool duplicates an incumbent's customer database. The hypothesis weakens if the restaurant relies on marketplace acquisition and cannot reconcile direct capacity. This scenario provides no proof that a chatbot would reduce staff effort.
