# Simulated scenario: neighbourhood restaurant

**SIMULATED — FICTIONAL.** No interview took place. Every profile detail and response below is invented; no real person, business, transaction or metric is represented. Prepared 2026-09-28 using the [interview guide](script.md).

## Profile sheet (fictional assumptions)

- Single independent restaurant in a residential Valencia neighbourhood; 36 seats.
- Owner also serves tables; two staff members may handle reservations.
- Lunch and dinner services; recurring local guests and some walk-ins.
- Current tools: paper diary, phone, WhatsApp Business app and a hosted menu PDF; a separate POS remains in use.
- Constraint: service-time updates must fit on a phone; replacing the POS is unattractive.

These are scenario parameters, not facts about the Spanish segment.

## Simulated conversation

**Question:** Walk through the latest changed reservation.

**Fictional response:** A group increased its party size by phone. One person changed the diary while another answered WhatsApp using the previous information. The conflict only became visible when the group arrived.

**Question:** What did you do next?

**Fictional response:** Staff moved tables and explained the wait. Nobody recorded the sequence of changes, so it was difficult to identify which information was current.

**Question:** Describe the last menu change.

**Fictional response:** A sold-out dish remained in the PDF until after service. The owner asked staff to explain it verbally; the printed and digital versions were no longer aligned.

**Question:** What happens when the software is unavailable?

**Fictional response:** The team needs a clear list for the next service and a way to reconcile later changes. A blank screen would make them return to the diary.

**Question:** What would make another tool unattractive?

**Fictional response:** Re-entering every booking, maintaining duplicate guest records, and asking all guests to join an account. A familiar diary can still be faster for a short service.

## Scenario-derived hypotheses, not findings

- A shared booking record with visible change history may reduce conflicting information.
- Fast menu availability changes may matter more than sophisticated merchandising.
- Recovery from unavailable or stale data is a core scenario, not a later polish task.
- Manual entry still needs a useful path; a direct web channel does not remove phone calls.

Public prompts: booking update/task friction [RM-001, 007, 010, 014](../review-ledger.md); [allergen-record rules](../legal.md). No public source confirms the fictional incidents above.

## What a real conversation must test

Ask for the actual latest conflict and how it was resolved; record booking volume and current update effort over a defined period. Look for counterexamples: a single booking channel, an incumbent that already synchronises reliably, or too few reservations to justify switching. This scenario provides no willingness-to-pay evidence.
